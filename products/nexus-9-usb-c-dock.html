Why it's built this way

  • 100 W pass-through. Charges a 16” laptop at full rate while every port stays live — 100 W in, up to 85 W to the host.
  • Dual displays, honestly rated. One monitor at 4K/60 Hz, or two at 4K/30 Hz. We publish both numbers instead of the flattering one.
  • Wired networking that holds. Gigabit Ethernet on a dedicated controller, so a video call doesn't collapse when the SD card is copying.
  • Milled aluminium shell. The enclosure is the heatsink. No plastic box quietly cooking the controller at 60 °C.
  • A cable that behaves. 30 cm braided, strain-relieved at both ends, long enough to reach and short enough to stay tidy.

Questions about the Nexus 9

Will it drive two 4K monitors at 60 Hz?

No — and no single-cable USB-C dock in this class honestly can without DisplayLink compression. Two 4K displays run at 30 Hz; a single 4K display runs at 60 Hz. Two 1440p displays also run at 60 Hz.

Does it work with an M-series MacBook?

Yes. Note that Apple silicon MacBook Air and base-model Pro machines support one external display natively, which is a laptop limitation rather than a dock one. Both HDMI ports are usable on M1/M2/M3 Pro and Max machines.

Do I still need my laptop's charger?

You need a USB-C power adapter plugged into the dock's PD input — the dock passes that power to the laptop. Your existing 96–100 W charger works; the dock does not ship with one, which keeps the price where it is.

Is the cable replaceable?

The cable is captive by design — detachable cables are the most common failure point in docks. If it fails inside the warranty period, the unit is replaced.
